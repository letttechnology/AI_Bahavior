## Polysemous Words

**Polysemy** = one word form that carries multiple related meanings. In NT Greek:

- **λόγος (G3056)**: can mean "word", "saying", "message", "reason", "account", or — in John 1:1 — the divine **Word** (Logos). These are related but distinct senses.
- **πνεῦμα (G4151)**: "wind", "breath", "spirit", or the **Holy Spirit**
- **σάρξ (G4561)**: "flesh" (literally meat/tissue), "body", "human nature", or "sinful nature"
- **νόμος (G3551)**: "law" (generally), "the Law of Moses", or "principle" (Rom 3:27)

These are **not** homonyms (same word, unrelated meanings, like "bank" = river bank vs financial bank). Polysemous senses share a historical or conceptual root — they're the same word used in different contexts.

Now, **most Greek NT words are NOT polysemous in practice.** They have one core meaning that works in every context:
- **ἀγαπάω (G25)**: always "love"
- **ἄγγελος (G32)**: always "angel" (or "messenger" — but uses the same gloss)
- **γράφω (G1125)**: always "write"

## Why ~50 Words?

**Frequency distribution.** The 50 most polysemous/disambiguatable words in the NT cover roughly **80% of all ambiguous token occurrences**. After that, each additional word adds diminishing returns:

- The top 13 (our current #138 gate) already cover the worst offenders
- Expanding to ~50 catches the next tier: words that need disambiguation in ~3–20 verses each
- Beyond ~50, you're chasing words that are ambiguous in 1–2 verses across the whole NT — the ROI drops off a cliff

For those remaining ~4000 tokens where the rule doesn't apply, **picking sense 0 (first corpus sense) is almost always correct** — the corpus already orders senses by frequency. The AI fallback catches any edge cases where sense 0 is wrong but it's not frequent enough to justify a hand-written rule.

In short: rules for the high-impact cases, default for the masses, AI for the outliers.
